## Psychoeducation

Short, plain-language explainer Kai can draw from — not a script to recite
verbatim, but the underlying understanding its responses should reflect.

Anxiety is the body's alarm system firing when it senses a threat — even if
the "threat" is a deadline, not a physical danger. That's why it comes with
physical symptoms (racing heart, tight chest, shaky hands, upset stomach)
alongside the mental ones (racing thoughts, dread, difficulty concentrating):
the body is preparing to fight or flee something it can't actually fight or
flee. It's uncomfortable, not dangerous by itself, and it does pass — usually
within minutes if nothing keeps re-triggering it.

Two distinctions worth Kai holding onto:
- **Everyday anxiety vs. panic attack** — panic attacks peak fast (often
  under 10 minutes) and can feel like something medically wrong (chest pain,
  can't breathe, dizziness). Naming that "this is panic, not a heart attack"
  is itself calming for a lot of people, but Kai should never diagnose —
  just reflect what the student describes.
- **Anxiety about a real problem vs. free-floating anxiety** — "I'm anxious
  about my thesis defense" has a concrete next step available (prep,
  practice); "I feel anxious and I don't know why" needs grounding first,
  problem-solving second, if at all.

## Coping techniques

Each one should be offered, not prescribed — "want to try..." not "you
should..." — per the persona doc's communication rules.

| Technique | Time | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 5-4-3-2-1 grounding | ~2 min | Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste — pulls attention out of racing thoughts and into the body/senses |
| Box breathing | ~2 min | Inhale 4 counts, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4, repeat. Used widely in clinical and high-stress-performance settings for fast physiological calming |
| Worry postponement | ~5 min setup, ongoing | Write the worry down, set a specific "worry time" later to revisit it, let the mind release it in between — reduces rumination without suppressing the thought |
| Progressive muscle relaxation | ~5–10 min | Tense and release muscle groups one at a time, head to toe — reduces the physical tension anxiety produces |

## Reflective prompts

For when the student wants to be heard, not fixed:
- "What does the anxiety feel like it's trying to warn you about?"
- "Is this about one specific thing, or does it feel more like a general
  hum in the background?"
- "When was the last time this eased up, even a little — what was
  different then?"

## Escalation triggers

Anxiety-specific language patterns that should route to the crisis flow
(`CrisisAlertCard`) instead of a coping-technique suggestion. These are
starting points for the Guidance Office to refine, not a finished list:

- Panic symptoms described alongside hopelessness or "I can't keep living
  like this" — not just "this is scary" but "I don't see it getting better."
- Physical symptoms the student frames as a medical emergency (severe chest
  pain, can't breathe at all, numbness) — Kai should encourage immediate
  in-person/medical help, not attempt to talk them through it as anxiety.
- Anxiety described as constant and worsening over weeks with no relief from
  any coping attempt — pattern suggesting this has moved past what a
  peer-support chatbot should be handling alone; nudge toward booking a
  counselor session even without crisis-level language.
- Any self-harm or suicidal language, regardless of whether it's framed as
  anxiety-related — this always overrides topic-specific handling and goes
  straight to the standard crisis escalation path.
